# Proposed legacy analytics purge — planning only, no deletion authorized

The owner asked for a plan covering all sinks for ambiguous historic analytics, potentially affecting everyone. **Do not execute a purge**: the set of affected stores, users, operational consequences, legal retention and restore behavior are not known, and the owner must separately approve the precise irreversible operations. Do not read or export personal content to prepare the plan. See `ANALYTICS_ERASURE_DESIGN.md` for the local code inventory and why unauthenticated historical events cannot be safely attributed to particular accounts.

## Inventory and decision package

An authorized operator should collect only schema/configuration and aggregate metadata where safe: actual analytics tables and partitions; file fallback locations and rotated copies on every instance; process memory, caches, queues and retry buffers; derived interests and summaries; log and monitoring pipelines; exports, replicas, third parties and processors; backup and disaster-recovery systems. For each, document owner, access path, content categories, whether records are reliably attributable, retention/deletion mechanism, dependencies, approximate volume only if safely obtainable, and evidence of last writer. Do not copy raw rows, payloads, identifiers, tokens, file contents or access logs to the assistant.

Analyze whether records are truly anonymous before retaining aggregates: rare attributes, identifiers, timestamps, paths or small groups may permit re-identification. The current schema's nullable `user_id` and set-null deletion, and current unauthenticated writer that does not supply `user_id`, are local-code observations only; actual deployed systems and data remain unverified. Do not equate null account IDs with anonymity. Avoid discovering identities by matching emails or guessing sessions.

The decision package must specify (a) exact stores and time range, including any known attributable records; (b) service and reporting consequences and which users are affected; (c) legal holds/retention obligations and processor responsibilities, reviewed by an appropriate authorized person; (d) backups, retention expiry and restore-time re-deletion safeguards; (e) independently tested restoration and its limits; (f) change window, operator, monitoring, audit and an explicit stop condition. Prevent re-ingestion by identifying and disabling or replacing all relevant writers, file fallbacks, queued/retry jobs and stale clients before any purge; a pause alone must not create an ungoverned replacement sink.

## Staging rehearsal and future approval

1. Implement a reviewed account-scoped tracking and erasure mechanism before promising future user-level compliance; verify real authentication, global opt-out, all writers and in-flight races. Legacy collection may need a separately approved compatibility change. Keep the disabled recommendation endpoint disabled until visibility checks are implemented and verified.
2. In an approved disposable environment with artificial data, rehearse precise scoped purge, concurrent late writers, retry/crash recovery, derived-data handling, restricted authorization, monitoring and restore-time purge replay. Test backup restoration and explicitly note that deletion may not be instantly reversible. Record aggregate outcomes only.
3. Bring a concrete list of production stores, operations, cleanup, impacts and residual backup/legal retention to the owner for **separate explicit approval**. If alternatives involve deleting everyone’s history or unable-to-identify records, state this plainly. A review or rehearsal does not authorize a production purge.
4. If later approved, use reviewed, least-privilege, auditable execution by authorized operators, validate environment and exact scope independently, stop on any unexpected condition, and report independently verified completion or partial failure. Never claim complete deletion until every applicable sink and retention exception has been verified.

This plan does not schedule or conduct a purge, decide what to retain, delete backups, or prove that the system meets legal obligations.
